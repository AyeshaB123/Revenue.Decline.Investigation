# Why did Revenue Decline by 43%?

---

## Executive Summary

To identify the primary driver, Revenue was decomposed into its two business components—**Average Selling Price (ASP)** and **Quantity Sold**. The analysis showed that ASP decreased by only **2.2%**, while Quantity Sold declined by **41.9%**, confirming that reduced sales volume was the primary cause of the decline.

A progressive drill-down analysis was then performed across **time, product hierarchy and geography dimensions**. The investigation narrowed its scope to the **Regular product class** during **April, May, June, July, and October**, where most lost sales occurred. Within this scope, **five product categories explained 98.9%** of the Quantity Sold decline, while **nine product subcategories accounted for 86.7%**. Geographic analysis further revealed that **North America contributed 81.8%** of the decline, with the **United States accounting for 94.4%** of the regional decrease.

This investigation provides a focused roadmap for business decision-making. Rather than applying organization-wide corrective actions, management should prioritize detailed investigation of the Regular product class, the highest-impact product categories and subcategories, and the North American market, where the majority of lost sales were concentrated. The next phase should determine whether the decline was driven by inventory constraints, pricing decisions, promotional effectiveness, changing customer demand, competitive pressure, sales execution, or broader market conditions. Identifying these underlying causes will enable targeted recovery strategies supported by measurable business evidence.

# Next Steps & Further Investigation

## Inventory & Supply Chain

**Objective:**

Determine whether product availability issues contributed to lost sales.

### Key Questions:

- Were affected products out of stock during high-decline months?
- Were stockouts concentrated in specific regions?
- Did supplier delays affect product availability?
- Were warehouse distribution issues impacting sales?

---

## Marketing & Promotions

**Objective:**

Evaluate whether changes in marketing activities affected customer demand.

### Key Questions:

- Were marketing campaigns reduced or discontinued?
- Did promotional activities become less effective?
- Was advertising investment reduced compared with 2011?
- Did competitors launch stronger promotional campaigns?

---

## Sales Operations

**Objective:**

Assess whether sales execution contributed to lower performance.

### Key Questions:

- Did sales team performance decline?
- Were there changes in sales territories or staffing?
- Were incentive structures changed?
- Did store-level operational changes impact sales?

---

## Customer Behavior

**Objective:**

Understand whether customer purchasing patterns changed.

### Key Questions:

- Did customer acquisition decline?
- Did customer churn increase?
- Did customers shift toward different product categories?
- Did repeat purchases decrease?
- Did customer satisfaction change?

---

## Market Demand

**Objective:**

Determine whether external market conditions influenced product demand.

### Key Questions:

- Did overall demand decline for affected categories?
- Did competitors gain market share?
- Did customer preferences change?
- Did economic conditions influence purchasing behavior?
- Was the United States market affected by broader demand changes?


## Conclusion

This project demonstrates a complete, evidence-driven approach to root cause analysis. Instead of treating "revenue decline" as a single vague problem, it was broken down into measurable components.

From there, a structured drill-down across time, product hierarchy, and geography narrowed a company-wide issue into a specific, actionable scope. Five product 
categories, nine subcategories, and one dominant market (the United States) accounted for the vast majority of the decline.

The outcome is not just a set of numbers, but a clear roadmap: management can now investigate a defined subset of products and geography, rather than searching 
blindly across the entire business. This reflects the core goal of analytics work: turning a broad, ambiguous question into a focused, testable path forward.
